Gas vs. electric: how to compare the cost per mile

The useful comparison is not simply what a gallon of gas costs beside a kilowatt-hour of electricity. It is how much energy each vehicle needs to travel the same distance.

If you are weighing a gas car against an electric vehicle, start with a normal week of driving. A national average can be a helpful reference, but your local gas station, utility rate, vehicle efficiency and charging routine are what shape your own bill.

1. Work out the gasoline cost

Divide the miles you drive by your vehicle’s miles per gallon (MPG). That gives you the gallons used. Multiply the gallons by the price you actually pay per gallon.

Example: Driving 12,000 miles in a car that gets 30 MPG uses about 400 gallons. At $3.50 per gallon, the estimated fuel bill is $1,400 for the year, before any changes in driving conditions.

2. Estimate the electricity cost

For an EV, divide annual miles by the vehicle’s miles per kilowatt-hour (mi/kWh). Multiply that energy use by your electricity price. Your utility bill may have a time-of-use rate, and public fast charging can cost more than home charging.

For example, 12,000 miles at 3.5 mi/kWh takes roughly 3,429 kWh. At $0.16 per kWh, that is about $549 in electricity. Real-world results vary with weather, speed, terrain, cabin heating or cooling and charging losses.

3. Compare like with like

  • Use the same annual mileage for both vehicles.
  • Use your actual local gasoline price and electricity rate.
  • Check the efficiency rating for the exact vehicle and model year.
  • Consider how much charging will happen at home versus public stations.

Energy is only one part of ownership

A lower energy bill does not automatically make one vehicle cheaper overall. Purchase price, financing, insurance, registration, tires, maintenance, depreciation and home charger installation can all affect the total. Compare those separately rather than assuming the energy calculation answers everything.
